What ACT 3 AI promises, and what it does not

ACT 3 AI does not use Your Content to train, fine-tune or evaluate any AI model, ours or anyone else's, and does not sell or license it for that purpose (Terms of Service §8.1). We may use service usage data, such as which features are used, error reports and credit use, in aggregated or de-identified form to run and improve the Service; it does not include the creative substance of your work (§8.2). If we ever offer a training program, it will be separate, opt-in and off by default (§8.4).

That promise is ACT 3 AI's own. It does not cover what an AI company does with the content ACT 3 AI sends it to run a feature you use (§8.3 and §9.4). Some of those companies' terms let them keep content, review it, or use it to improve their models. Where a company offers us a setting or agreement that excludes your content from training or limits retention, we use it where it is available to us; the table says where that is not yet confirmed.

Protecting actors and confidential projects

  1. Get written consent from every real person whose face or voice you upload, covering the AI models your film uses.
  2. Check the table row for every model your film uses, including voices and upscalers.
  3. For a confidential project, choose models whose row says No, and avoid the ones marked "Yes, unless opted out", "Depends on plan" or "Unknown".
  4. For an actor who refuses AI training, give that character an open-source voice run by ACT 3 AI.
  5. Ask ACT 3 AI before staff-only tools (such as Cinematography Superintelligence) run on your project.
