Voice watermarks

Some voice-related tools will include a watermark for the purpose that it be identified as AI. "PerTh (Perceptual Threshold) neural watermark". EU AI Act's Article 50.

Why they put them in

  • Legal implications for selling a movie or commercial use: The watermark does NOT mean someone else owns your movie or the audio. It is a transparency/provenance mark, not a copyright claim or license restriction that transfers ownership.
  • Provenance / accountability: So the audio can later be identified as AI-generated (and, in Chatterbox's case, from that system). This is especially relevant for voice-cloning models that can produce realistic deepfakes.
  • Regulatory compliance: The EU AI Act's Article 50 (transparency obligations, applying from around 2 August 2026, with a grace period for marking on older systems until roughly 2 December 2026) requires providers of systems that generate synthetic audio (and image/video/text) to mark outputs in a machine-readable way so they are detectable as artificially generated. Imperceptible watermarks are one of the main technical approaches being used for this.
  • Responsible AI positioning: Resemble deliberately ships the watermark by default in the open-source Chatterbox code.

Copyright in AI-generated material

Whether AI-generated material is protected by copyright is a separate legal question from these contracts. See:

  • U.S. Copyright Office: Copyright and Artificial Intelligence (Part 2 Copyrightability report, registration guidance)
  • U.S. Copyright Office: Copyright Registration Guidance for works containing AI-generated material (March 16, 2023)
  • Thaler v. Perlmutter, D.C. Circuit opinion (March 18, 2025)
  • UK Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988, section 9 (computer-generated works)
  • EU AI Act Article 50: transparency and labelling of AI-generated content (AI Act Service Desk)
  • Beijing Internet Court judgment on an AI-generated image, Li v. Liu (2023), English translation
