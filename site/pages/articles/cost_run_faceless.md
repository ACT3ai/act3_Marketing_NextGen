# Cost to Run a Faceless AI YouTube Channel in 2026

**Short answer:** a weekly 15-minute faceless AI YouTube channel with every shot generated costs about $1,342 to $1,933 a month in 2026, before music licensing. The habit that decides where you land is attempts per shot.

A slideshow over narration, or one generation per shot with nothing ever rejected, would cost far less. This guide prices four fully generated episodes a month, with retries, voice, thumbnails and the final render included. For choosing a format and building the workflow, see [how to run a faceless YouTube channel with AI video](/articles/run_faceless_ai). This page is about the monthly bill, and how to cut it.

## How do you calculate a faceless channel's monthly cost?

```
per finished shot = attempts × credits per generation
                  + first frames × credits per image
                  + generated seconds per shot × 72
monthly credits   = episodes × shots per episode × per finished shot
                  + voice + thumbnails
monthly dollars   = monthly credits ÷ 500
```

The 72 is ACT 3 AI's final-render rate in credits per second of generated footage, as quoted before you confirm, and $1 buys 500 credits. For the same shot-based arithmetic applied to films, see [how much an AI movie costs to make](/articles/ai_movie_cost).

The inputs:

| Input | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Episodes | 4 a month, 15 minutes (900 seconds) each |
| Shots | 6-second average shot: 900 ÷ 6 = 150 per episode, 600 a month |
| Generated seconds per shot | 8, trimmed to the 6 you use |
| Video rate | Seedance 2.0 Fast 720p: 314 credits per 8-second shot ($0.628) |
| First frames | 2 images per shot at 25 credits (Nano Banana 2, 1K) |
| Voice | 14,000 characters per episode (about 2,250 words) at 225 credits per 1,000 |
| Thumbnails | 4 options per episode at 25 credits |
| Final render | One per episode, priced on the footage in the final cut |
| Lean habits | 1.5 attempts per shot, 1 voice pass |
| Typical habits | 3 attempts per shot, 2 voice passes |

Cut faster and the shot count rises: 180 shots at 5 seconds, 300 at 3 seconds. Every line except voice and thumbnails scales with it.

Per finished shot, lean is 1.5 × 314 + 2 × 25 + 8 × 72 = 1,097 credits ($2.194), and typical is 3 × 314 + 2 × 25 + 8 × 72 = 1,568 credits ($3.136). Per episode, that is $335.60 lean and $483.20 typical.

Two things stand out. First, the final render is just over half the lean bill and over a third of the typical one, because it is quoted on generated seconds, not the trimmed cut: an 8-second generation used as a 6-second shot is priced at all 8. Second, the $590.40 gap between columns is neither the model nor the script. Both use the same model and the same 600 shots. Doubling attempts and voice passes is what adds 44 percent to the bill.

## What costs do faceless channels forget?

* **The render.** 4,800 generated seconds a month at 72 credits is quoted at 345,600 credits ($691.20). Because it is priced on generated length, an over-long generation costs twice: once to generate and again to render.
* **The scrapped episode.** Abandon one after generating but before rendering, and the lean column loses a quarter of its motion, first-frame and voice lines: 81,300 credits ($162.60).
* **Script changes after voice.** A 14,000-character pass is only 3,150 credits ($6.30), but shots cut to the old timing often need regenerating. See [adding TTS voiceover and lipsync](/articles/adding_voiceover_ai).
* **Thumbnails at 4K,** which cost twice the 1K rate.
* **Music licensing.** Generated video does not license a soundtrack. Budget an example rate of $10 to $30 a month for a creator music subscription, on top of the range above.
* **Storage** for a growing library of takes and renders. Pro includes 740 GB; Blockbuster Edition includes 3.3 TB.
* **Your time.** Hand-writing 150 prompts a week is a part-time job with no invoice.

## How do you cut the monthly bill?

**1. Cut attempts first.** One extra attempt per shot is 150 shots × 4 episodes = 600 extra generations a month, or 188,400 credits ($376.80). Approve a still first frame, at 25 credits, before paying 314 for motion; composition, wardrobe and likeness can all be judged on the still. See [how to auto-generate first frames for every shot](/articles/first_ai_automatically).

**2. Review before you render.** Fix shots on the timeline before running the final render, and lock approved shots so they are not regenerated by accident.

**3. Generate the length you cut.** Seedance 2.0 generates 4 to 15 seconds. At 6 generated seconds, the render is quoted on 600 × 6 = 3,600 seconds, or 259,200 credits ($518.40), saving 86,400 credits ($172.80) a month. Generation cost scales with duration too, so motion falls as well.

**4. Draft cheap.** Seedance 2.0 Mini 480p costs 73 credits ($0.146) per 8-second shot, under a quarter of Fast 720p. If Mini 720p at 157 credits is good enough to publish, lean motion becomes 900 × 157 = 141,300 credits ($282.60), half the Fast figure.

**5. Reuse locations and characters.** A recurring narrator-character and a few recurring settings get rejected far less than a new world every week, and they make the channel recognisable.

## The test worth running

Produce one episode exactly the way you plan to make every episode. Total the credits it used and divide by its shot count: that is your real cost per finished shot. Near 1,097 means you are working lean; above 1,568 means attempts need fixing before you buy a bigger plan. Multiply the episode total by four, divide by 500, and compare the result with the plan table. Now you are budgeting from your own channel, not from assumptions.
