## What actually differs between the tiers?

Three things are verified: price, resolution and maximum shot length.

**Resolution.** Mini and Fast come at 480p and 720p, Seedance 2.0 at 480p to 4K, and Seedance 2.5 at 480p to 1080p, so 4K Seedance means Seedance 2.0.

**Shot length.** The whole Seedance 2.0 family, Mini and Fast included, runs 4 to 15 seconds. Seedance 2.5 runs 4 to 30 seconds and is the longest single shot among the video models ACT 3 AI offers. A model snaps a shot to its nearest allowed length, so a 20-second take is either one Seedance 2.5 shot or two shots from the 2.0 family.

For long takes, price scales with length. As a straight-line estimate, not a quoted price, a 30-second Seedance 2.5 1080p shot would be about 2,128 × 30 ÷ 8 = 7,980 credits ($15.96).

**What is not verified** is how the tiers compare on motion, detail or faces. Treat any claim that one tier "looks better" as something to check on your own footage; the test at the end of this page does it for about $24.

## Which tier should you use for drafts, and which for finals?

Use the cheapest tier that can answer the question you are asking right now.

A draft settles questions you can judge at low resolution: does the action fit the length, does the camera move read, is the shot worth finishing. Mini 480p, at 9.125 credits a second, is the cheapest place to ask. Fast 480p costs twice as much; whether that is worth it is a test result, not a given.

A final is the take you deliver, so run it at the tier and resolution you ship: Seedance 2.0 1080p, Seedance 2.5 1080p or Seedance 2.0 4K.

## When do cheap drafts beat a full-quality pass?

Drafting pays when the final attempts it saves per shot exceed draft credits ÷ final credits.

Three Mini 480p drafts cost 3 × 73 = 219 credits. Against Seedance 2.0 1080p that is 219 ÷ 1,946 = 0.11, so the drafts pay off if they prevent one rejected final in every 9 shots (1,946 ÷ 219 = 8.9). Against Seedance 2.5 1080p it is 219 ÷ 2,128 = 0.10, one prevented final in every 10 shots (2,128 ÷ 219 = 9.7).

That bar is low, but drafting does not win everywhere:

* **Routine shots.** If the shot usually lands first time, drafts are pure cost.
* **Problems a draft cannot show.** Fine detail, a face or the delivered look are judged at the resolution you ship. Settle composition and wardrobe on a still first frame instead; see [the cost of locking references versus regenerating video](/articles/cost_locking_references).
* **Too many drafts.** Ten Mini 480p drafts are 730 credits, 38% of a Seedance 2.0 1080p final (730 ÷ 1,946 = 0.375). Stop when a draft stops telling you anything new.
