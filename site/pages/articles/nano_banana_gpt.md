## When is the pricier image model worth it?

Going from Nano Banana 2 to Nano Banana Pro adds 73 credits per still (98 − 25). Going from Nano Banana 2 to GPT Image 2.0 adds 60 (85 − 25). The upgrade pays for itself if it prevents enough rejected video takes. Break-even, assuming one still per shot:

* **Seedance 2.0 1080p (1,946):** Nano Banana Pro must save one rejected take in every 26 shots (1,946 ÷ 73 = 26.7); GPT Image 2.0 one in every 32 (1,946 ÷ 60 = 32.4).
* **Grok Imagine 720p (364):** Nano Banana Pro must save one in every 5 shots (364 ÷ 73 = 5.0); GPT Image 2.0 one in every 6 (364 ÷ 60 = 6.1).

## How do you test image models on your own shots?

We have no test data ranking these models on likeness, text rendering or detail, and any ranking would age with the next release. Test instead, on five briefs that stand for the shot types your film needs:

1. A three-quarter close-up of your lead in a named outfit, with the key light on a stated side.
2. The same character in a second outfit, to see whether the face holds.
3. A wide establishing shot with real geometry: where the door is, where the window is.
4. A reverse angle of the same room, to see whether the geography survives.
5. A two-person staging with a clear eyeline, or a product with a readable label if your film needs one.

Briefs 2 and 4 are the storyboard tests: a panel set has to read as one film.

Score adherence, not beauty. For each frame, count the explicit instructions it obeyed: angle, wardrobe, light direction, framing, background. A striking frame that ignored your angle is worse than a plain one that obeyed it, because your motion prompt builds on the angle.

## Should you use one image model for the whole project?

Not necessarily. If the test shows different models suiting different shot types, route by shot type. Say, for illustration, 60 of your 100 shots pass on Nano Banana 2 and 40 need Nano Banana Pro. One frame per shot costs 60 × 25 + 40 × 98 = 1,500 + 3,920 = 5,420 credits, against 9,800 for Nano Banana Pro everywhere. That saves 4,380 credits ($8.76), a 45% cut in frame spend, but only about 2.25 rejected Seedance 2.0 1080p takes (4,380 ÷ 1,946). Fewer motion rejections remain the bigger lever. If you mix models, hold lighting and grade constant so the shots cut together.
